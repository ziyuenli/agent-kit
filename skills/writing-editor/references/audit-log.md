# Rule Audit Log

This file records maintenance evidence and is not an active writing guide.

## EMAIL-001

- Category: email replies
- Observed behavior gap: A scheduling reply repeated availability after already stating flexibility and added a closing sentence that repeated the purpose of the exchange.
- Exact proposed wording: "In a reply, answer the recipient's current question directly. Do not restate details that are already established or add a closing sentence that only repeats the email's purpose."
- Intended scope: Replies to existing email threads, including scheduling and logistics.
- Boundary: Repeat a date, condition, request, or decision when it is needed to confirm, correct, or disambiguate the arrangement.
- Duplication and conflict check: No active email rule covers replies. The shared preference against repetition is broader and does not define this email-specific decision.
- Positive behavior case: After saying the sender can work around the recipient's schedule, omit a second sentence saying that both proposed mornings are acceptable.
- Negative behavior case: If only one proposed morning is possible, state that exact availability rather than relying on a general expression of flexibility.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-002

- Category: research-contact emails
- Observed behavior gap: A draft named a specific paper and year to demonstrate that the recipient's research had been checked, although the paper itself was not important to the message. The user wanted the research to be verified first and then summarized naturally.
- Exact proposed wording: "In a research-contact email, do not name a paper or year merely to show that the recipient's research was checked. Name a specific work when it is itself the reason for contact or is needed to distinguish the intended research connection."
- Intended scope: Emails that establish research fit with a professor, researcher, group, or prospective collaborator.
- Boundary: Name a paper when that specific work is itself the reason for contact or is needed to distinguish the intended research connection.
- Duplication and conflict check: The original verification clause duplicated shared factual-accuracy guidance and was removed. `EMAIL-001` governs repetition in replies; neither rule determines whether publication details belong in a research-contact email.
- Positive behavior case: Describe the recipient's verified work on spatially varying responses to environmental drivers without inserting the supporting paper title or publication year.
- Negative behavior case: If the inquiry proposes extending one specific published method, identify that paper or method clearly enough for the recipient to know which work is meant.
- User decision and date: Revised and approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-003

- Category: email tone
- Observed behavior gap: Several drafts sounded academic or machine-written and used unnecessarily elevated wording.
- Exact proposed wording: "Use plain, natural language in emails. Keep the tone professional without making it academic or ceremonial."
- Intended scope: All drafted or revised emails.
- Boundary: Retain technical terms and conventional formal wording when they are needed for accuracy or expected by the recipient.
- Duplication and conflict check: Shared guidance already requires plain, natural language. The recipient and requested tone provide enough context for professional wording, so a separate email rule is not justified.
- Positive behavior case: Use direct, conversational sentences while retaining an appropriate professional greeting and request.
- Negative behavior case: Do not replace an established technical term with a casual paraphrase that changes its meaning.
- User decision and date: Rejected as duplicative of shared guidance 2026-09-11.
- Current status: rejected

## EMAIL-004

- Category: recipient research fit
- Observed behavior gap: Drafts catalogued a recipient's research areas or repeated generic expertise shared by both sender and recipient instead of identifying the distinctive point of connection.
- Exact proposed wording: "When explaining why the recipient is relevant, select the one or two aspects of their work that directly connect to the sender's purpose. Do not catalogue their research areas, repeat an obvious shared field, or use generic praise in place of the actual connection."
- Intended scope: Academic research-contact emails that explain why a particular researcher or group was chosen.
- Boundary: Include more than two aspects when the request genuinely depends on several distinct parts of the recipient's work and each one changes the proposed connection.
- Duplication and conflict check: `EMAIL-002` governs verification and whether to name papers. This rule governs selection and relevance after the research has been verified.
- Positive behavior case: For an InSAR recipient, identify the relevant large-scale landslide assessment project rather than praising their general SAR expertise.
- Negative behavior case: If a proposed visit connects separate imaging and hazard-modelling strands, state both rather than forcing them into one vague phrase.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-005

- Category: explaining the sender's research
- Observed behavior gap: A draft listed phase linking, phase unwrapping, and publication details without first explaining the practical problem that motivated the work or what the methods made possible for a recipient outside the same subfield.
- Exact proposed wording: "When the recipient is outside the sender's exact subfield, explain the sender's research through the problem, the reason it matters, and what the work now makes possible. Include method details only when they help the recipient understand the contribution or judge the fit."
- Intended scope: Research-contact emails to recipients who may not share the sender's narrow technical background.
- Boundary: Use more technical detail when writing to a specialist in the same area or when the recipient has asked for it.
- Duplication and conflict check: The base editing contract preserves meaning and facts but does not specify how to make specialized work intelligible across fields.
- Positive behavior case: Explain that the methods recover otherwise missing movement information across forested slopes before naming the relevant InSAR techniques.
- Negative behavior case: Do not replace a method with a broad benefit statement when the method itself establishes the sender's technical fit.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-006

- Category: compressing technical lists
- Observed behavior gap: Drafts enumerated every environmental input, method, or research topic even when an accurate umbrella phrase would have conveyed the same point more clearly.
- Exact proposed wording: "In a research-contact email, replace a list of methods, datasets, factors, or research topics with an accurate summary phrase when the individual items do not help the recipient understand the request or assess the fit."
- Intended scope: Academic research-contact emails containing technical or research background.
- Boundary: Keep individual items when they define the proposed work, distinguish it from the recipient's work, or are needed to prevent an inaccurate generalization.
- Duplication and conflict check: Shared guidance favors concise, plain writing but does not define when detailed research lists should be compressed. This rule gives the specific decision demonstrated by repeated list-heavy drafts.
- Positive behavior case: Use `multimodal geospatial and meteorological data` instead of listing terrain, drainage, geology, land use, roads, and rainfall when the list adds no decision value.
- Negative behavior case: List the exact available data sources when their availability determines whether the proposed project is feasible.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-007

- Category: statements of interest and familiarity
- Observed behavior gap: A draft used `have been following` when the sender only wanted to express current, specific interest, creating an unsupported impression of long-term familiarity.
- Exact proposed wording: "Do not imply long-term familiarity with the recipient's work unless the sender has established it as true. When only current interest is known, state the specific interest directly."
- Intended scope: Academic research-contact emails that describe the sender's interest in or familiarity with a recipient's work.
- Boundary: Preserve a statement of long-term interest when the sender explicitly provides it as a fact.
- Duplication and conflict check: Shared factual-accuracy guidance applies generally, but this recurring email phrase creates a specific interpersonal claim that is easy to overstate during polishing.
- Positive behavior case: Use `I was particularly interested in your work on...` when only the present research connection is established.
- Negative behavior case: Do not weaken `I have followed your research for several years` when the sender explicitly says that this is true.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-008

- Category: requests and relationship stance
- Observed behavior gap: Drafts presumed future collaboration, suggested an unverified joint arrangement with another specialist, or used language that sounded too equal or forward when writing to a senior professor.
- Exact proposed wording: "Ask about interest, availability, or a possible next step without presuming the recipient's agreement, a future collaboration, or an unconfirmed joint arrangement. Match the relationship language to what has actually been established in the exchange."
- Intended scope: Academic cold emails, visiting inquiries, follow-ups, and replies about possible research collaboration.
- Boundary: Use direct collaborative language after the recipient has agreed to collaborate or when an existing collaboration is already established.
- Duplication and conflict check: `EMAIL-001` removes repetitive reply wording. This rule governs unsupported relationship assumptions and the level of directness in the request.
- Positive behavior case: Ask whether the direction may be of interest and whether the professor might be open to hosting a visit.
- Negative behavior case: After a collaboration has been agreed, do not retreat to language that treats it as merely hypothetical.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active

## EMAIL-009

- Category: research-contact email structure
- Observed behavior gap: Drafts obscured the purpose, overextended the research background, or separated the proposed direction from the decision being requested.
- Exact proposed wording: "In a research-contact email, state the purpose and requested arrangement in the opening, then establish the focused research connection and the sender's relevant foundation or next direction. End with one clear, low-pressure next step."
- Intended scope: First-contact research emails, including visiting-research inquiries.
- Boundary: In a reply, answer the recipient's current question rather than rebuilding the structure of the original inquiry.
- Duplication and conflict check: `EMAIL-001` governs replies. `EMAIL-004` and `EMAIL-005` govern the content of the fit paragraphs; this rule only governs their order and the explicit request.
- Positive behavior case: Open with the visiting inquiry and timing, explain the research connection, and close by asking whether a discussion would be possible.
- Negative behavior case: Do not repeat the full opening and research history when replying only to schedule a call.
- User decision and date: Approved 2026-09-11.
- Current status: active
