# Email Rules

## General email replies

- Answer the recipient's current question directly. Do not restate details that are already established or add a closing sentence that only repeats the email's purpose.
- Repeat a date, condition, request, or decision when needed to confirm, correct, or disambiguate the arrangement.

## Research-contact emails

- Do not name a paper or year merely to show that the recipient's research was checked. Name a specific work when it is itself the reason for contact or is needed to distinguish the intended research connection.
- When explaining why the recipient is relevant, select the one or two aspects of their work that directly connect to the sender's purpose. Do not catalogue their research areas, repeat an obvious shared field, or use generic praise in place of the actual connection.
- When the recipient is outside the sender's exact subfield, explain the sender's research through the problem, the reason it matters, and what the work now makes possible. Include method details only when they help the recipient understand the contribution or judge the fit.
- Replace a list of methods, datasets, factors, or research topics with an accurate summary phrase when the individual items do not help the recipient understand the request or assess the fit.
- Do not imply long-term familiarity with the recipient's work unless the sender has established it as true. When only current interest is known, state the specific interest directly.
- Ask about interest, availability, or a possible next step without presuming the recipient's agreement, a future collaboration, or an unconfirmed joint arrangement. Match the relationship language to what has actually been established in the exchange.
- In a first-contact research email, state the purpose and requested arrangement in the opening, then establish the focused research connection and the sender's relevant foundation or next direction. End with one clear, low-pressure next step. In a reply, answer the recipient's current question rather than rebuilding the original inquiry.
